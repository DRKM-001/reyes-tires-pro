How to read a tire size

Every tire has a size printed on the sidewall, for example 225/45R17. Each part of that code tells you something about the tire.

225/45R17
225width (mm)
45aspect ratio (%)
Rradial
17wheel (in)

The parts, one by one

PartMeaning
225Section width of the tire in millimeters, from sidewall to sidewall.
45Aspect ratio: the sidewall height is 45% of the width. A lower number means a shorter, stiffer sidewall.
RRadial construction, used on nearly all modern passenger tires.
17Wheel diameter in inches. The tire must match the wheel.

Load index and speed rating

After the size you may see a code like 94V. The number is the load index, the maximum weight one tire can carry. The letter is the speed rating. Use the codes printed on your tire or in your owner's manual, and never go below the manufacturer's specification.

Where to find it

Look at the side of any tire on your car. The size is in raised letters near the rim. It is also on the sticker inside the driver's door frame, which lists the size your car came with.

Other numbers on the sidewall

DOT date code: the last four digits show when the tire was made. "2421" means week 24 of 2021. Tires age even when they are not driven, so check this on used tires.

Changing sizes? A different size can affect speedometer accuracy, ride, and clearance. Stay within the sizes your vehicle's manufacturer lists, or ask a tire professional before you change.
